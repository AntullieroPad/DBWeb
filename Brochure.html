So what do you do with all this?

We're not telling you not to go. Puerto Rico is extraordinary — its people, its food, its music, its history, its resilience. It deserves to be experienced, celebrated, and loved.

We're telling you to go differently.

A short checklist for the ethical Yale tourist:

  • Stay at a locally owned guesthouse, not a chain hotel or AirBnB
  • Eat at locally owned restaurants — not the ones on TripAdvisor's "Best Of" list
  • Learn at least a little of the history before you land
  • Spend money in communities outside Old San Juan and the tourist corridor
  • Do not refer to Puerto Rico as "basically like going international"
  • Remember that Puerto Ricans are U.S. citizens denied self-determination for 126 years
  • Come back — not just for spring break
Puerto Rico is not a destination. It is a nation under occupation that happens to have beautiful beaches.

When you leave — and you will leave, because you can — do not say "Puerto Rico was healing."

It was not sick. You were.

Congratulations on your trip

You've chosen one of the Caribbean's most beautiful, vibrant, and — let's be honest — affordable destinations. Have you ever wondered why it's so affordable?

Puerto Rico is not "underdeveloped." It is over-managed. It is not "chaotic." It is over-governed from elsewhere. It is not "full of potential." It has been systematically redirected so that its potential accrues elsewhere.

But don't let that dampen your mood. The beaches are stunning.

The Beach™ — Rated

Puerto Rico's beaches are constitutionally public. Every single one. The law says no one can own them.

At some point you will notice something unsettling: the beach is pristine, the cafés are minimalist, the neighborhood feels curated. What you are experiencing is the end stage of a familiar process: declare a place paradise, price out the people who made it livable, replace culture with branding, call it revitalization.

And yet, the law still says no one can own them.

Did you know?
Puerto Rico's constitution guarantees public access to all beaches. Despite this, luxury hotels and private developments have systematically restricted coastline access for decades. The view from your hotel balcony used to belong to everyone.
Where to Stay™

Accommodation options in Puerto Rico have never been more plentiful — or more complicated.

The AirBnB question

Short-term rentals are often framed as innovation. In practice, they function as a slow, polite eviction. Entire neighborhoods — Old San Juan, Rincón, coastal zones — have been converted into rotating inventories for people who will not stay. Housing becomes an experience. Residents become externalities.

If a place becomes easier for you to access, it usually becomes harder for someone else to remain.

The Act 20/22 question

In 2012, Puerto Rico passed tax incentive laws attracting wealthy U.S. mainland investors with near-zero capital gains rates. Local Puerto Ricans — who do not qualify — watched their neighborhoods transform around them. Some of these transplants also went to schools like Yale. Small world.

On culture

Puerto Rican culture is not a brand asset. It is not a vibe. It is not a backdrop for personal growth arcs.

Enjoyment does not require ignorance. But ignorance makes enjoyment cheaper.

Did you know?
The median household income in Puerto Rico is ~$22,000. The average Yale graduate's starting salary exceeds $70,000. When you visit Puerto Rico, you are not a tourist. You are an economic actor. Act accordingly.
Who Owns Paradise™?

In 2016, the U.S. Congress passed PROMESA — creating an unelected fiscal oversight board with the power to override decisions made by Puerto Rico's democratically elected government.

La Junta's mandate: ensure Puerto Rico pays its creditors. What it has overseen: closure of hundreds of public schools, cuts to pensions, reductions in healthcare funding, and the restructuring of $70 billion in debt.

"PROMESA es pobreza."
That's not a slogan. It's a diagnosis.
The Yale Connection

Yale trains people to analyze systems, extract insight, and move on. Fluent in critique. Allergic to implication. Yale's endowment has historically held investments in Puerto Rican debt instruments through hedge fund managers who sued the Puerto Rican government to ensure repayment.

The institution that gave you the education that pays for this trip has financially benefited from the conditions that make it affordable. We thought you should know.

Did you know?
Puerto Rico's debt crisis is not the result of overspending. It is the result of decades of U.S. federal policy that prohibited Puerto Rico from filing for bankruptcy and allowed Wall Street to sell it predatory bonds. The island didn't borrow its way into crisis. It was structured into one.
