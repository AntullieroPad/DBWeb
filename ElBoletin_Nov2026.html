Estamos Aquí

Puerto Rican Futurity Conference 2027 · In the Halls of Power

April 23–24, 2027 · CENTRO, Hunter College · New York

We have the venue, the catering, and a vision. Now we need speakers and funding.

The program is built around four connected clusters:

  • EducaciónCluster I

    UPR budget cuts, the suppression of student activists, access to higher education, and brain drain.

  • PractitionersCluster II

    Climate, infrastructure, food sovereignty, water, and energy.

  • MultigeneracionalCluster III

    Conversations with the elders who came before us: people passing on their movements, their trains of thought, and the wisdom it takes for a community to succeed together.

  • Youth futuresCluster IV

    Why so many young people struggle to imagine a future on the island — and what it would take to change that.

PolíticaIn the room, at the polls

El Voto Es Nuestra BalaOct 30

We organized El Voto Es Nuestra Bala to make civic power practical: help with voter registration, absentee ballots, and state-specific voting questions, alongside a bigger conversation about what political participation can and can't do for our communities. Voting isn't the whole of political power, but knowing how to use the system is part of it.

It was great to have State Representatives Juan Candelaria and Christopher Rosario, Junta for Progressive Action, and others in the room with us. Thank you to our co-presenters, Yale Democrats and the Yale Progressive Party.

Beyond BordersMEChA de Yale · Nov 7

DB collaborated with Diaspora Pa'lante at MEChA de Yale's Beyond Borders mini-conference, where Diaspora Pa'lante led a teaching on the neocolonialism Puerto Rico faces today. Thank you to MEChA for including us.

A note from Antonio

As someone who worked all summer to make this happen, I'm incredibly proud — not just of myself, but of my community for being willing to support such a vision. I know Yale has a tendency to take away your dreams. But I think it's important to hold onto them. When you show up for community, community shows up for you.

I'm a senior. I'm heading out and thinking about my post-Yale plans. But I wanted to leave a gift for everyone before I go, and this is it. I won't ever get to enjoy it myself. I would have pursued this had there been a recognized curriculum at Yale, so I'm giving others what I would've wanted.

I also have a larger commitment to our history being taught. For too long, we have wanted Puerto Rican Studies at Yale, but there was no foundation for it to stand on. This is my way of building that infrastructure. Many of the issues our island faces are shared with the wider region, and I think we tend to forget that. Despite being distinct and unique, we share more with our neighbors than not.

There's a real possibility we get denied this year. If that happens, next year is when we need to fight hardest, because Yale will only give us this when it sees us fighting for it.

— Antonio Padilla, Coalition Chair

Repatriación TaínaThe ancestors at the Peabody

The research is done and the alliances are in place. Now we wait.

  1. DoneResearch & alliances
  2. DoneProposal shared with Taíno partners
  3. NowAdvisory board of elders decides
  4. NextRequest to the Peabody

DB has shared a written proposal with our Taíno community partners, who will bring it to an advisory board of elders and leaders from tribes across the Caribbean. If the board moves forward, it will submit a request for voluntary repatriation to the Peabody.

Our role in this work is to do research, connect people, and use our access to Yale. Decisions about representation and the return of the ancestors belong to Taíno communities. Efforts like this usually take three to five years.
